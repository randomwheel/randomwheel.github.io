How to play Solitaire

Solitaire is a single-player card game built around patience, planning, and a little luck. The most popular version is Klondike, the format used by many classic computer card games. The objective is to move all fifty-two cards into four foundation piles, one foundation for each suit. Each foundation begins with an ace and grows upward through two, three, four, and the remaining cards until the king is placed.

The tableau is the main working area. It contains seven columns with cards arranged in descending lengths. Only the top card of each column is face up at the beginning. You can place a face-up card on another card when it is one rank lower and the color is opposite. For example, a red eight can be placed on a black nine. Groups of cards can move together when they are already arranged in a legal alternating-color sequence.

Empty tableau spaces are important. A vacant column can only be filled with a king, or with a sequence that begins with a king. This rule makes it valuable to avoid opening a column too early unless you already know which king will occupy it. When no useful move remains, draw from the stock. Depending on the variation, you may draw one card or three cards, and the waste pile can provide new opportunities.

Solitaire strategy tips

Good Solitaire play is less about moving the first available card and more about choosing the move that reveals information. Whenever possible, expose a face-down tableau card. A move that uncovers a hidden card usually has more value than a move that simply rearranges two visible cards. Revealed cards increase your choices and help you identify the missing ranks needed for a foundation.

Build foundations steadily, but do not rush every low card upward. A two or three may still be needed in the tableau to support a sequence. Before sending a card to a foundation, check whether it is blocking a useful alternating run. Experienced players constantly compare short-term progress with long-term flexibility.

Use empty spaces with intention. Moving a king into an empty column can open a large sequence, but the move may also hide access to another card. If two kings are available, prefer the king that lets you uncover the most face-down cards or creates the strongest alternating sequence. In draw-three games, remember the order of cards in the waste instead of clicking randomly.

Why Solitaire is a popular browser game

Solitaire works beautifully online because a round can last a few minutes or become a longer relaxing session. There is no account, download, or opponent to wait for. The quiet pace makes it suitable for a short break, while the decision-making keeps each deal interesting. It also offers a satisfying blend of order and surprise: the rules are simple, but every deal asks you to adapt.

Playing in a browser makes the game convenient on desktop, tablet, and mobile screens. Use a larger screen when you want to study several columns at once, or tap the cards from a phone when you are traveling. The important design goal is clarity. Cards should be easy to distinguish, buttons should be readable, and a new deal should always be one tap away.

Frequently asked questions

Is this Solitaire game free?

Yes. This page is designed as a free browser game with no installation required. Advertising areas may appear around the content, while the game itself remains available to play.

What is the goal of Klondike Solitaire?

The goal is to move all cards to four suit foundations, starting with aces and building upward in rank until each foundation contains a king.

Can I play Solitaire on a phone?

Yes. The layout is responsive and the controls are designed for touch-friendly play on modern phones and tablets.

What should beginners do first?

Look for moves that reveal face-down cards, preserve empty columns for kings, and avoid using the foundation as quickly as possible when a card may still help in the tableau.
