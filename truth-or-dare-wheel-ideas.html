01. Why a Spin Wheel Makes Truth or Dare Better

Traditional Truth or Dare has a flaw: the person asking always controls the game. They target the same people, use the same questions, and the tension between friends can get awkward. A spin wheel removes that dynamic entirely.

  • Fairness: Everyone has an equal chance of being selected — no favouritism
  • Speed: No awkward deliberation. Spin, act, repeat.
  • Variety: Pre-loaded prompts mean no one has to think on the spot
  • Customisation: Add your own inside jokes and personal dares for ultimate personalisation

04. How to Set Up Your Truth or Dare Wheel Game

The setup takes under two minutes. Here's the format that works best for groups of 4–12 people:

🎮 The Ultimate Truth or Dare Wheel Format

  1. 1Set up two wheels: one with everyone's names, one with "Truth" or "Dare" segments (adjust ratio to your group's preference — 50/50 is classic).
  2. 2Spin the name wheel first to pick a player. That person spins the Truth/Dare wheel.
  3. 3Depending on the result, the group picks a prompt from the matching list (or uses a third wheel loaded with prompts).
  4. 4Each player gets one "skip" per game — no questions asked. This keeps things fun without pressure.
  5. 5Anyone who completes a dare earns a point. Most points at the end of the night wins bragging rights!

For online game nights over video call, a shared browser spin wheel is the perfect solution. One host shares their screen with the wheel loaded, and the spun player responds via camera. It works surprisingly well on Zoom, Discord, and FaceTime — the video reactions to dares are often the highlight of the whole night.

Pro tip: Use Discord's "share audio" option when screen-sharing so everyone hears the satisfying wheel-spin tick sound. It adds to the suspense enormously.
